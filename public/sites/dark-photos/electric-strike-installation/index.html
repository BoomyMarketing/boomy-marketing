In short

Electric strike or magnetic lock: which does my door need?

For doors getting phone or card access

An electric strike replaces the strike plate in the door frame and releases the latch when it gets a signal. A magnetic lock holds the door shut with an electromagnet and releases when its power is cut. SmartphoneKey’s reader switches either one with its built-in relay. Which suits your door, and how people leave safely, is decided at the door check.

Two kinds of electric lock

How they differ at the door.

General differences; your door, frame and exit needs decide the choice.

Electric strike

  • Sits in the door frame in place of the strike plate.
  • Releases the door’s latch when switched.
  • Works with the existing lockset and handle.

Magnetic lock

  • Mounts on the frame with a plate on the door.
  • Holds the door with an electromagnet while powered.
  • Needs a safe way out, such as the exit button in the box.

Is it the right fit?

Good for many. Not for every door.

A good fit if

  • The door already has an electric strike or magnetic lock.
  • You want to add phone keys to a door that only takes a regular key, and you’re ready for wiring.
  • You need a record of entries at that door.

Maybe not if

  • You want no wiring at all. The reader and lock need low-voltage power.
  • You can’t get approval to change the door or frame.

Answers

What people ask first.

Straight answers, including the ones that aren’t a simple yes.

Does the reader work with my existing electric strike?

The reader’s relay is designed to switch an electric strike or magnetic lock. The door check confirms your lock, power supply and cable route before anything is promised.

What voltage does it need?

SmartphoneKey’s guide lists 12–24 V DC for the reader, ideally from the same supply as the lock.

Is a magnetic lock safe for an exit door?

A magnetic lock always needs a safe way out. An exit button is included, and exit and safety needs are confirmed on site before installation.

What happens in a power cut?

It depends on the lock. A magnetic lock releases when it loses power; an electric strike stays locked or releases, depending on the model. Which suits your door is decided at the door check.

How long does installation take?

It depends on the door. A door that already has an electric lock and power nearby is a smaller job than one that needs a lock, power and a new cable route. You get the timing with the cost after the door check, before any work starts.

I rent. Can I still do this?

Talk to your landlord or property manager first: changing a door needs the owner’s approval, and a shared entrance is the building’s decision (in a condo, the board’s). Owners decide for their own doors; condo-unit owners should check the building’s rules.
