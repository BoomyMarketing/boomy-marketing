How it works

Tap. Open. Manage.

Three parts: a key on the phone, a reader beside the door, and an admin view for whoever runs the place.

Tap

People hold a phone with a Wallet key, or a SmartphoneKey key card, to the reader. SmartphoneKey describes Wallet keys as working without a separate app.

Open

The reader’s built-in relay switches the door’s electric lock: an electric strike or a magnetic lock. The lock does the physical work.

Manage

In SmartphoneKey’s admin you add and remove keys, send guest keys with a start and end time, and review an activity log of entries.

The reader

A slim reader. Your door does the rest.

It mounts beside the door, about the height of a phone, and switches the door’s electric lock. Here is what it does and what it needs.

  1. 1

    Tap area

    A phone with a Wallet key, or a SmartphoneKey key card, is held here.

  2. 2

    QR code for visitors

    Visitors scan it with their phone camera to call the right person. SmartphoneKey says no app is needed. Two extra QR stickers in the box can go wherever visitors will see them.

  3. 3

    Electric lock

    A relay built into the reader switches an electric strike or a magnetic lock. Which one suits your door is decided on site.

  4. 4

    Low-voltage power

    SmartphoneKey’s guide lists 12–24 V DC, ideally from the same supply as the lock.

  5. 5

    Exit button

    A push-to-exit button for the inside comes in the box. The installer confirms how people leave safely.

Wi-Fi model

Joins your network during setup.

LTE model

Uses a SIM card, for doors where Wi-Fi isn’t practical.

SmartphoneKey also describes Matter support over Wi-Fi or Thread for smart-home setups.

Kinds of keys

One door. Many kinds of key.

Each person gets the access that fits them. These are the options SmartphoneKey describes.

Wallet key
Stored in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet. It works until you remove it.
Key card
A physical NFC card for people who don’t want to use a phone. Cards are programmed and revoked in the admin.
Guest key
Has a start and an end time and can be limited to a set number of uses. It is sent as a link, by email or message.
QR visitor call
Visitors without a key scan the QR code at the door to call the right person.
Lock schedule
SmartphoneKey’s admin guide describes weekly access times for each lock.
Removal and history
Any key can be removed. The activity log shows keys accepted, doors unlocked, and keys added or removed.

From SmartphoneKey’s features page, installation and usage guide, and admin guide. A door check confirms what applies to your door.

Answers

Straight answers.

What people ask first, and what we won’t overpromise.

Is this SmartphoneKey’s own website?

No. This site is run by an independent local installer of SmartphoneKey and covers sales and installation in Toronto and the GTA.

Do people need an app?

SmartphoneKey describes Wallet keys as working from Apple Wallet or Google Wallet without a separate app, and guest keys as links. Whoever manages access uses SmartphoneKey’s admin.

Will it work with the lock I have?

The reader switches electric locks, such as electric strikes and magnetic locks. A door check confirms your lock, power and wiring. Doors that only take a regular key usually need hardware added first.

What if Wi-Fi doesn’t reach the door?

There is an LTE model that connects over a mobile network with a SIM card. It needs mobile signal at the door.

Can I see who came in?

SmartphoneKey’s admin guide describes an activity log of keys accepted, doors unlocked and keys added or removed, which can be filtered by lock, date and user.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of doors and keys and on any electrical work, so there is no price on this site. Cost is explained after the door check, including any ongoing costs.

Which page is right for me?

Pick the one that matches who runs the door: gyms and studios, condos and buildings, offices and coworking, salon suites and rental rooms, short-term rentals or homes.

What if a phone battery is flat?

SmartphoneKey doesn’t promise entry with a dead phone, so every setup plans a backup. An NFC key card works on the same reader, and the door check settles another way in for your door.

What happens in a power cut?

The reader and the electric lock run on low-voltage power. In an outage, a magnetic lock releases, and an electric strike stays locked or releases depending on the model chosen. How your door should behave, and how people get out, is decided at the door check; ask about a backup power supply there too.

Is a phone key secure?

Keys sit in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet on each person’s own phone, can be limited to set times and can be removed at any moment. The activity log shows every entry, and a separate audit log shows which admin issued or removed which key.

Who can see the entry records?

Entry records stay in SmartphoneKey’s admin portal: an activity log of keys accepted and doors unlocked, and an audit log of admin changes. You decide who is an admin and who can see which camera. The local installer’s privacy terms will be published before launch.

How long does installation take?

It depends on the door. A door that already has an electric lock and power nearby is a smaller job than one that needs a lock, power and a new cable route. You get the timing with the cost after the door check, before any work starts.

I rent. Can I still do this?

Talk to your landlord or property manager first: changing a door needs the owner’s approval, and a shared entrance is the building’s decision (in a condo, the board’s). Owners decide for their own doors; condo-unit owners should check the building’s rules.
