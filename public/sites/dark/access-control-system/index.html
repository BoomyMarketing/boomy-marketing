In short

What is an access control system?

For business owners, property and facility managers

An access control system decides who can open which door, and when. With SmartphoneKey, a reader beside each door accepts phone keys from Apple Wallet or Google Wallet and NFC key cards, then switches the door’s electric lock. You add, time-limit and remove access in one admin portal, and every entry is logged.

What you control

Who, which door, and when.

The parts of SmartphoneKey that matter most when several people share several doors.

  • Keys for every person

    Wallet keys for staff and residents, NFC key cards for anyone who would rather not use a phone, and guest keys with a start and end time.

  • Door by door

    One key can cover several doors, and each lock can follow a weekly schedule of the hours it accepts keys.

  • Sites and local admins

    Group doors by building or location and let a manager run one site without touching the others.

  • Activity and audit logs

    Every key accepted and door unlocked, plus a separate record of which admin issued or removed which key.

  • Remove access at once

    Remove a key, or deactivate a person: their door access ends and their portal sign-in is disabled.

  • Cameras and visitors

    Connect ONVIF-compatible cameras with camera roles, and let visitors scan a QR code on the reader to call.

From SmartphoneKey’s features page, installation and usage guide, and admin guide. A door check confirms what applies to your door.

The door check

What we check at your door.

SmartphoneKey switches electric locks. Whether your door already has one, or needs one, decides what the job involves.

  1. 1

    The lock you have

    Regular key only, an electric strike, a magnetic lock, or an existing fob or buzzer system?

  2. 2

    Power at the door

    The reader and the lock need low-voltage power nearby.

  3. 3

    The cable route

    How wiring gets from the reader to the lock and the power supply.

  4. 4

    How people leave

    An exit button is included. Exit and safety needs are confirmed on site.

  5. 5

    Wi-Fi or LTE

    Dependable Wi-Fi at the door, or the LTE model with a SIM card.

  6. 6

    Who needs in

    Everyone who needs a key, who only visits, and who would rather use a card.

Is it the right fit?

Good for many. Not for every door.

A good fit if

  • People join and leave often, and keys or fobs keep going missing.
  • You run more than one door or more than one site.
  • You want a record of who came in and which admin changed what.

Maybe not if

  • You need access created automatically from HR, membership or booking software. That link isn’t confirmed.
  • A door can’t take an electric lock and low-voltage power.

Answers

What people ask first.

Straight answers, including the ones that aren’t a simple yes.

Does it work with the locks we already have?

The reader switches electric locks: an electric strike or a magnetic lock. If a door already has one, the door check confirms the lock, power and wiring. A door that only takes a regular key usually needs an electric lock added first.

Can we keep our current fobs or cards?

Whether an existing fob system stays, or the reader replaces it, is decided on site. SmartphoneKey uses its own NFC key cards alongside Wallet keys.

Can one person manage several buildings?

Yes. Doors and people can be grouped into sites, and an admin switches between them from one sign-in. Local admins can run a single site.

Where is it managed from?

From SmartphoneKey’s admin portal in a browser on a computer or phone. You can sign in with email, Google or Apple.

Is a phone key secure?

Keys sit in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet on each person’s own phone, can be limited to set times and can be removed at any moment. The activity log shows every entry, and a separate audit log shows which admin issued or removed which key.

Who can see the entry records?

Entry records stay in SmartphoneKey’s admin portal: an activity log of keys accepted and doors unlocked, and an audit log of admin changes. You decide who is an admin and who can see which camera. The local installer’s privacy terms will be published before launch.

What happens in a power cut?

The reader and the electric lock run on low-voltage power. In an outage, a magnetic lock releases, and an electric strike stays locked or releases depending on the model chosen. How your door should behave, and how people get out, is decided at the door check; ask about a backup power supply there too.

How long does installation take?

It depends on the door. A door that already has an electric lock and power nearby is a smaller job than one that needs a lock, power and a new cable route. You get the timing with the cost after the door check, before any work starts.
