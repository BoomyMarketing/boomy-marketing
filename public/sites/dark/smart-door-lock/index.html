In short

What kind of smart lock is SmartphoneKey?

For homeowners, landlords and small businesses

SmartphoneKey is not a battery deadbolt. It is a slim reader mounted beside the door that reads phone keys from Apple Wallet or Google Wallet and NFC cards, then switches a powered electric lock: an electric strike or a magnetic lock. That makes it a smart lock setup for doors many people use, installed after a door check.

Two kinds of smart lock

Battery deadbolt or reader with an electric lock?

Both open without a metal key. They suit different doors.

Battery smart deadbolt

  • Replaces the deadbolt on the door itself.
  • Runs on batteries you change.
  • Usually set up with the lock maker’s app.
  • Often a do-it-yourself install.

Reader with an electric lock (SmartphoneKey)

  • A reader beside the door switches an electric strike or a magnetic lock.
  • Runs on low-voltage power, 12–24 V DC per SmartphoneKey’s guide.
  • Keys in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet, NFC cards and timed guest links.
  • Installed after a door check: lock, power, cable route and Wi-Fi or LTE.

Is it the right fit?

Good for many. Not for every door.

A good fit if

  • Several people, sitters or trades use the door.
  • You want guest access that ends by itself and a record of entries.
  • You’re happy to have an electric lock and power installed.

Maybe not if

  • You want a lock you fit yourself, with no wiring.
  • You rent and the owner or building hasn’t approved changes to the door.

Answers

What people ask first.

Straight answers, including the ones that aren’t a simple yes.

Which smart lock is best for a front door?

It depends on the door and who uses it. A battery deadbolt suits a simple do-it-yourself job. A reader with an electric lock suits doors with many users, guests or trades, and comes with timed keys and an entry log.

Does it replace my deadbolt?

Not necessarily. The reader switches an electric strike or a magnetic lock. The door check decides which suits your door and what happens to the existing hardware.

Does it work with Google Home or Apple Home?

SmartphoneKey describes Matter support over Wi-Fi or Thread. Which hub and devices you use is confirmed at the door check.

Can it go on a condo unit door?

Your own unit door and the building’s entrance are separate decisions. Check the building’s rules first; the shared entrance is the board’s or manager’s call.

What if a phone battery is flat?

SmartphoneKey doesn’t promise entry with a dead phone, so every setup plans a backup. An NFC key card works on the same reader, and the door check settles another way in for your door.

What happens in a power cut?

The reader and the electric lock run on low-voltage power. In an outage, a magnetic lock releases, and an electric strike stays locked or releases depending on the model chosen. How your door should behave, and how people get out, is decided at the door check; ask about a backup power supply there too.

Is a phone key secure?

Keys sit in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet on each person’s own phone, can be limited to set times and can be removed at any moment. The activity log shows every entry, and a separate audit log shows which admin issued or removed which key.

I rent. Can I still do this?

Talk to your landlord or property manager first: changing a door needs the owner’s approval, and a shared entrance is the building’s decision (in a condo, the board’s). Owners decide for their own doors; condo-unit owners should check the building’s rules.
