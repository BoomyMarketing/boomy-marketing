Which door?

Your own door and the building’s entrance are different decisions.

Most building requests start here, because it decides who can say yes.

The shared entrance

Lobby, garage and amenity doors belong to the building. The owner, the property manager or the condo board decides, and building approvals come first.

  • Existing intercom or fob panels are part of the conversation.
  • A setup is sized by shared doors and keys, not by the number of suites.
  • Exit and fire requirements are confirmed on site.

A private unit door

A resident can only consider their own unit door, and building rules still apply: the door type, fire requirements and whether changes are allowed at all.

  • Check the building’s rules before planning anything.
  • The lobby stays under the building’s own system.
  • The homes page covers a single unit door.

Resident, not manager? Buying a key can’t change the lobby door. Share this page with your property manager or board instead.

Your entrance

What we check at a shared entrance.

SmartphoneKey switches electric locks. A shared entrance usually has an existing system around it, so the check starts with what is already there.

  1. 1

    What’s on the entrance now

    Intercom panel, fob reader, electric strike or magnetic lock, and what should stay or change.

  2. 2

    Power at the door

    The reader and the lock need low-voltage power nearby.

  3. 3

    Wiring and other doors

    The cable route, plus which garage and amenity doors should be included.

  4. 4

    Exit and fire requirements

    Exit hardware and safety needs are confirmed on site before anything changes.

  5. 5

    Wi-Fi or LTE

    A dependable network at the entrance, or the LTE model with a SIM card.

  6. 6

    Who approves

    The owner, manager or board, and any building approvals the change needs.

Suites aren’t doors. A large building may have only a few shared entrances, so a setup is sized by doors and keys, not by the number of suites.

Answers for buildings

What managers and boards ask first.

Including the questions that need a site visit to answer properly.

Can a resident put this on the lobby door?

No. Shared entrances are the building’s decision: the owner, property manager or condo board, with any approvals the building needs. A resident can only consider their own unit door, within the building’s rules.

Does it replace our intercom?

SmartphoneKey describes its QR code as a digital intercom: visitors scan it to call the right person. Whether that can replace your current intercom depends on the building and what the system has to do, so the door check covers it.

What about residents without a smartphone?

SmartphoneKey key cards work on the same reader. They are programmed and revoked in the admin.

What happens when someone moves out?

Their key is removed in the admin and stops working. The activity log records keys added and removed as well as entries.

How is pricing worked out for a large building?

By shared doors and keys, not suites, plus any electrical work. Package details are confirmed before a quote; there is no price on this site.

We already have a fob system.

An existing system usually means electric locking hardware is in place, but that doesn’t make it compatible. The door check confirms what can stay and what would change.

We manage more than one building.

SmartphoneKey’s admin guide describes separate entities for each building or site, each with its own doors, users and local admins.
