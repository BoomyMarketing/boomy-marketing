In short

What is phone access for a condo entrance?

For property managers, condo boards and building owners

Residents open the shared entrance with a key in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet. Visitors scan a QR code on the reader to call the person they are visiting. The property manager adds, finds and removes residents from one list, and every entry is logged, so move-outs no longer mean chasing keys and fobs.

What you get

The features that matter for condos & buildings.

  • Keys

    Apple Wallet & Google Wallet keys

    The key lives in the phone’s Wallet. People tap it on the reader, with no separate app for everyday entry.

  • Visitors

    QR intercom at the door

    Visitors scan the QR code on the reader with their phone camera. It opens in the browser and connects them to the right person to ask for entry.

  • Keys

    One key, several doors

    A single key can cover more than one lock, for example a lobby and a unit door, or a front door and a back door.

  • People & sites

    People directory

    Find anyone by name, email, phone, unit number or licence plate. Search by a unit number and that unit comes first.

  • People & sites

    Bulk import

    On larger plans, add many people at once from a CSV or Excel file.

  • People & sites

    Local admins

    Let a manager, front desk or superintendent run one site. You can hand them a set number of Wallet Keys to give out.

  • People & sites

  • Records & control

The door check

What we check at your door.

SmartphoneKey switches electric locks. Whether your door already has one, or needs one, decides what the job involves.

  1. 1

    The lock you have

    Regular key only, electric strike, magnetic lock or an old fob system.

  2. 2

    Power at the door

    The reader and the lock need low-voltage power nearby.

  3. 3

    The cable route

    How wiring reaches the lock and the power supply.

  4. 4

    How people leave

    An exit button is included; exit and safety needs are confirmed on site.

  5. 5

    Wi-Fi or LTE

    Dependable Wi-Fi at the door, or the LTE model with a SIM card.

  6. 6

    Which door

    The shared entrance, a unit door, or both. The shared entrance is the building’s decision.

Live in a unit? Your own door is yours to change. The lobby, garage and other shared doors belong to the building, so the board or manager decides.

Is it the right fit?

Good for many. Not for every door.

A good fit if

  • You manage a building or several, with residents moving in and out.
  • You want visitors handled at the door without a wired buzzer panel.
  • You want one list of who has access, searchable by unit.

Maybe not if

  • The shared entrance can’t take an electric lock or power.
  • You need access to sync automatically from another property system. That isn’t confirmed.

Answers

What condos & buildings ask first.

Straight answers, including the ones that aren’t a simple yes.

Can residents keep using fobs?

Residents who prefer a card can get an NFC key card that works on the same reader.

How do visitors get in?

They scan the QR code on the reader with their phone camera and connect to the person they’re visiting to ask for entry. No app needed.

We have hundreds of residents.

Larger plans let you import people from a CSV or Excel file, and the directory finds anyone by unit number, name, phone or licence plate.

Can the superintendent manage keys?

Yes. Make them a local admin for the building, with their own set of Wallet Keys to give out.

What does it cost for a building?

It depends on the doors, locks, power and how many people need keys. The local team explains setup and cost after the door check.
