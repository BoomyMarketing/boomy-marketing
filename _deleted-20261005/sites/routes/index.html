Stations: people and their access problems

One door. Many people.

Before choosing hardware, map who comes through the door. Every station on this line is a person with a different access problem.

  1. Members

    Need the door at the hours you allow, without a key of their own.

  2. Coaches

    Start, cover shifts and move on. Access should follow them, not a key ring.

  3. Cleaners

    Arrive after everyone has gone. They need their own permission, not the spare.

  4. Guests

    Change every stay. A new person needs a new permission, and an end to it.

  5. Contractors

    In for the job. Grant access for the work and revoke it when the work is done.

G

Route G · the main line, first and thickest

Gyms & studios

Open early. Hand over. Lock up late.

Stations on route G (4)

  1. G1Early opening

    The first coach opens up before anyone else arrives. Grant them digital permission for the door instead of handing a key over the night before.

  2. G2New coach

    Starts on Monday. Grant a phone credential: no key to cut, no copy to track.

  3. G3Departing coach

    Leaves on Friday. Revoke their digital permission that day. Any physical key they still hold may still open the door; that depends on the lock and cylinder left in place.

  4. G4Late cleaning

    The cleaning crew comes after close. Give the people who clean their own permission, and revoke it if the contract ends.

+Grant

When someone starts, give their phone digital permission for the door.

−Revoke

When someone leaves, take that permission away. There is nothing to collect from their phone.

Not promised: automatic sync with your membership software. Plan to manage phone credentials yourself unless a specific connection is confirmed for your setup.

Route B · residential and mixed-use buildings

Buildings

Common doors. Move-outs. Contractors.

Stations on route B (3)

  1. B1Common doors

    Residents, staff and trades all use the shared entrances, each person with their own permission.

  2. B2Move-outs

    Someone hands back a unit. Revoke their digital permission; nobody else’s access has to change. Any physical key they kept needs a separate check; revoking phone access does not disable it.

  3. B3Contractors

    A trade crew for a week. Grant access for the job, then revoke it, instead of a key copied and left in a van.

Change one person. Leave everyone else alone.

Permissions are granted and revoked person by person, so a move-out doesn’t mean re-issuing access to the whole building.

Junction · all five routes pass through here

Three real parts, on every route.

  1. A wall-mounted reader

    Fixed to the wall beside the door. It’s where people use their phone to get in.

  2. A suitable electric lock

    The reader releases an electric lock or strike that suits the door. Mechanical-only doors need one fitted, with power and wiring.

  3. Managed phone credentials

    Digital permissions you grant to a person’s phone and revoke when they no longer need the door.

GBROH

Platforms · separate ways in

Three platforms. Three different ways in.

Wallet, app and visitor QR calling are separate methods, not one feature with three names. Each is checked against your phones, your door and your configuration before anyone relies on it.

1
Platform 1

Wallet

For people you’ve granted access who’d rather use their phone’s built-in wallet.

  1. You grant the credential.
  2. It is added to their phone’s wallet, where supported.
  3. They use their phone at the reader.

Check first: which phones and wallets your people use, and whether wallet credentials are available for your configuration.

2
Platform 2

App

For people you’ve granted access who use the app on their phone.

  1. You grant the credential.
  2. They set up the app on their phone.
  3. They use it at the reader.

Check first: phone operating systems, app setup for each person, and who manages the accounts.

3
Platform 3

Visitor QR calling

For visitors who don’t hold a credential of their own.

  1. The visitor scans the QR code at the entrance.
  2. It starts a call to someone who can let them in.
  3. The person answering decides.

Check first: who receives the calls and when, and how visitor calling is configured at your entrance.

Gym, building, rental, office or home: the work depends on the door, not the route. There are no prices on this page because the door decides them.

The assessment

E

Electronic already

There is a card reader, fob, keypad or electric lock on the door today.

  • What the existing lock or strike is, and whether it suits the reader.
  • Power supply and the condition of existing wiring.
  • Where the wall-mounted reader goes.

Parts that suit may stay; parts that don’t are replaced. No connection to your current system is assumed.

M

Mechanical key only

A key cylinder is the only way in today.

  • Door and frame condition for a suitable electric lock or strike.
  • Power near the door and a route for wiring to the reader.
  • Where the wall-mounted reader goes.

Expect electric-locking and wiring work before phone access works.

!

Check the old physical keys.

Revoking someone’s digital access does not disable a physical key they still hold. Decide during the assessment what the old keys should still open and whether the cylinder needs changing.

Plain guide

The interactive guide needs JavaScript. Here is the same logic in writing:

  1. Pick your route. G gyms and studios, B buildings, R rentals, O offices, H homes.
  2. Check the door. Electronic already: the existing lock, power and wiring are checked. Mechanical key only: expect a suitable electric lock or strike, power and wiring to a wall-mounted reader.
  3. List who needs access. Staff, members or residents, cleaners and contractors, guests: each gets a permission you grant and revoke.
  4. Qualify a method. Wallet, app and visitor QR calling each need their own compatibility and configuration check.
  5. Next step: a door assessment. Check what existing physical keys can still open. Revoking phone access does not disable those keys.
